How does raising the punishment for purchasing prostitution to a felony level affect sex-related crime rates?

Aidan Madsen, with Carly Pope and Clara Eccles

2021

Texas H.B. 1540 takes effect

7

donor states in Synthetic Texas

11

years of FBI NIBRS data

Null

no statistically significant effect

01 The question

In 2021, Texas passed H.B. 1540, which made buying sex a state jail felony. Selling sex stayed a Class B misdemeanor. We asked whether the tougher penalty on buyers changed the rate of reported sex-related offenses in Texas.

02 The method

We used a synthetic control design in Stata. We built a “Synthetic Texas” from Illinois, Montana, North Dakota, Oklahoma, Oregon, Washington, and Wisconsin, weighted to match Texas over 2013–2020, then compared the two after the law took effect. We left out New York and Maine (their own 2021 prostitution law changes), Nevada (prostitution is legal there), and states with incomplete reporting.

03 The data

FBI National Incident-Based Reporting System (NIBRS) data for 2013–2023, covering prostitution, human trafficking, forcible rape, and pornography/obscene material offenses. Because states recorded prostitution offenses inconsistently, we combined them into one aggregate prostitution count.

04 What we found

No statistically significant effect. Reported offenses dipped right after the law and then ticked back up. Texas did not stand out from the donor states in placebo comparisons, and the gap between Texas and Synthetic Texas was under ten offenses per 100,000 people. We suspect changes in what gets reported and prosecuted explain more of the movement than changes in behavior.

05 Limits

NIBRS only captures reported crime, so a market pushed further underground could look like a drop. Texas and Synthetic Texas diverged in 2020 and just before the law, and we only have a short window after it. A null result here does not prove the law changed nothing.
